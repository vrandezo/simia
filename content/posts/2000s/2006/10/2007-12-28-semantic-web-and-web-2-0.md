I usually don't just point to other blog entries (thus being a bad
blogger regarding netiquette), but this time [Benjamin Nowack nailed it
in his post on the Semantic Web and
Web 2.0](http://www.bnode.org/archives2/63). I read the iX article (a
popular German computer technology magazine), and I lost quite some
respect for the magazine as there were so many unfounded claims,
off-the-hand remarks, and so much bad attitude in the article (and in
further articles scuttered around the issue) towards the Semantic Web
that I thought the publisher was personally set on a crusade. I could go
through the article and write a commentory on it, and list the errors,
but honestly, I don't see the point. At least it made me appreciate peer
review and scientific method a lot more. The implementation of peer
review is flawed as well, but I realize it could be so much worse (and
it could be better as well - maybe [PLoS](http://www.plos.org/index.php)
is a better implementation of peer review).

So, go to Benji's post and convince yourself: there is no *"vs"* in
Semantic Web and Web 2.0.
