Not everyone likes our proposal for the [Semantic
Wikipedia](Semantic_Wikipedia "wikilink"). That's not a big surprise
really. [Boris Mann](http://www.bmannconsulting.com/) was talking about
the [advantages of tagging](http://www.bmannconsulting.com/node/1544),
and some ideas like blessed tags, that sounded very nice, when [Jay
Fienberg](http://icite.net/about/people.html) pointed him to the
[Semantic MediaWiki](http://meta.wikimedia.org/wiki/Semantic_MediaWiki)
proposal. Boris answers: *"I notice with a shudder however, that the
Mediawiki stuff uses a large "S" Semantic, and includes RDF. I admit it,
I'm afraid of RDF."*

Yes, we do. And we're proud of it. Actually, it's the base for the
better half of the possible applications we describe. Jay has some nice
answers to it: *"I think the MediaWiki folks are just recognizing the
connection between their "tags" and the big "S" Semantic Web \[you
bet\!, denny\]. There are taxonomies and ontologies behind the popular
tagging apps too--folks behind them just aren't recognizing /
publicizing this (for a number of reasons, including that tags are often
part of a practical application without big "S" Semantic Web goals).
\[...\] I'm not a super huge fan of RDF myself, but I think it's useful
to not be afraid of it, because some interesting things will come out of
it at some point."*

Our idea was to allow the user to use Semantic Web technologies even
without really understanding them. No one needs to understand RDF fully,
or OWL, to be able to use it. Sure, if she does, well, it surely will
help her. Any by the way, RDF really is not complicated at all, it just
has a syntax that sucks. So what?

Maybe it's a crude joke of history to start the Semantic Web with
syntactic problems...
