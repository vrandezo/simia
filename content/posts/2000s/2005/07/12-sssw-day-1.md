Today's invited speaker was [Frank von
Harmelen](http://www.cs.vu.nl/%7Efrankh/), co-editor of the [OWL
standard](http://www.w3.org/TR/owl-features/) and author of the
[Semantic Web Primer](http://www.semanticwebprimer.org/). His talk was
on fundamental research challenges generated by the Semantic Web (or:
two dozen Ph.D. topics in a single talk). He had the idea after he was
asked one day in the cafeteria "Hey Frank, whazzup in the Semantic Web?"

In the tradition of Immanuel Kant's [four famous questions on
philosophy](http://members.optusnet.com.au/~gjmoses/KANT1.htm), Frank
posed the four big research challenges:

  - Where does the metadata come from?
  - Where do the ontologies come form?
  - What to do with the many different ontologies?
  - Where's the Web in the Semantic Web?

He derived many research questions that arise when you bring results
from other fields (like databases, natural language, machine learning,
information retrieval or knowledge engineering) to the Semantic Web and
not just change the buzzwords, but take the implications that come along
with the Semantic Web seriously.

The other talks were given by [Asun
Gómez-Pérez](http://webode.dia.fi.upm.es/Asun/Asun.html) on
[Ontological
Engineering](http://webode.dia.fi.upm.es/ontologicalengineering/), and
[Sean Bechhofer](http://potato.cs.man.ac.uk/seanb/) on Knowledge
Representation Languages for the SemWeb, pretty good stuff by the people
who wrote the book. I just wonder if it was too fast for the people who
didn't know about it already, and too repeting for the others, but well,
that's always the problem with these kind of things.

The hands-on session later was interesting: we had to understand several
OWL ontologies and explain certain inferences, and [Natasha
Noy](http://smi-web.stanford.edu/people/noy/) helped us with the new
[Protégé 3.1](http://protege.stanford.edu/). It was harder than I
thought quite some times. And finally [Aldo
Gangemi](http://www.loa-cnr.it/gangemi.html) was giving us some
exercises with knowledge representation design patterns, based on
[DOLCE](http://www.loa-cnr.it/DOLCE.html). This was hard stuff...

Wow, this was a lot of namedropping. The social programme (we were
hiking today) around the summer school, and the talks with the peers are
sometimes even more interesting than the actual summer school programme
itself, but this probably won't be too interesting for most of you, and
it's getting late as well, so I just call it a day.
