A philosopher with schizophrenia wrote a harrowing account of how he
experiences schizophrenia. And I wonder if some of the lessons are true
for everyone, and what that means for society.

  -   
    *"It’s definite belief, not certainty, that allows me to get along.
    It’s not that certainty, or something like it, never matters. If you
    are fixing dinner for me I’ll try to be clear about the eggplant
    allergy \[...\] But most of the time, just having a definite, if
    unconfirmed and possibly false, belief about the situation is fine.
    It allows one to get along.*

<!-- end list -->

  -   
    *"I think of this attitude as a kind of “intellectual humility”
    because although I do care about truth—and as a consequence of
    caring about truth, I do form beliefs about what is true—I no longer
    agonize about whether my judgments are wrong. For me, living
    relatively free from debilitating anxiety is incompatible with
    relentless pursuit of truth. Instead, I need clear beliefs and a
    willingness to change them when circumstances and evidence demand,
    without worrying about, or getting upset about, being wrong. This
    attitude has made life better and has made the “near-collapses” much
    rarer."*

<!-- end list -->
