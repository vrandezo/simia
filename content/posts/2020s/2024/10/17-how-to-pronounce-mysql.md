Today I learned (or re-learned), that the "My" in
[MySQL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MySQL) does not stand for the
English word [my](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Lexeme:L1367828) but for
the Swedish name My, which is the name of the daughter of MySQL
co-founder [Michael
Widenius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Widenius). ♡

The name My was introduced by [Tove
Jansson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tove_Jansson) for the
[Moomins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moomins) character [Little
My](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_My).

According to the [Words &
Stuff](https://web.archive.org/web/20240213140718/https://www.kith.org/words/2023/03/17/how-to-pronounce-finnish-moomin-names/)
blog: *"Turns out that she was named after the Greek letter mu; for the
Finnish pronunciation of Myy, see the video. In English, it turns out
that her name is pronounced like the English word my (/maɪ/), rhyming
with the English word hi."*

So you could pronounce My as `[ˈmyː]` or as `[maɪ]`.

This is in addition to the well-known discussion about [how to pronounce
SQL](https://patorjk.com/blog/2012/01/26/pronouncing-sql-s-q-l-or-sequel/),
which I will not further dive into here.

By the way, the MySQL documentation [defines the official
pronunciation](https://dev.mysql.com/doc/refman/8.4/en/what-is-mysql.html):
*"The official way to pronounce “MySQL” is “My Ess Que Ell” (not “my
sequel”), but we do not mind if you pronounce it as “my sequel” or in
some other localized way."*, but it seems that when speaking Swedish the
MySQL developers also say "mü-ess-ku-ell"
([source](https://forums.mysql.com/read.php?12,164918,276723#msg-276723)).
