I mean, it's Nolan. It's a great movie. He knows how to make movies. So
the three hours pass like a breeze and are enjoyable. The cast is great,
I particularly enjoyed Matt Damon, Robert Pattinson and Samantha Morton
as Circe.

Agamemnon was absolutely breathtaking. He stole every scene he was in.
(Until he started talking. I didn't like his voice. But those looks\!)

The first half hour or so of the movie felt like an extended trailer.
Which is very Nolan, but surprising and a little unsettling given the
familiar setting and story. It gets more settled and classical later on.

I really disliked the body horror scenes around Circe. And the design of
the monsters was... unsatisfying. Polyphem? Ok. Unusual and new. But
then Scylla with the same beige color and skin-like texture? It looked
like Ray Harryhausen. Which was fine in his time, but underwhelming in a
2026 AAA title. But it did give me a genuine jump scare.

I found the whole movie lacking color saturation. I have to compare it
to 2004 Troy, which I find much more appealing as a Greek mythology saga
movie. The Odyssey is a 2026 movie, binding it to our time in subtle
ways, on the expense of the timelessness that Troy has.

(Spoilers in this paragraph) But what I really enjoyed was the novel
twist to the plot, that I haven't seen before. That it was Odysseus who
broke the old contract that was keeping society together. That this was
the reason for the long way home. In a way this was the same core plot
point as Oppenheimer. And possibly an important lesson for our age. That
gave food for thought. But almost like a memory of the thoughts that
Oppenheimer provoked. But I enjoyed these ideas explored in such a
familiar setting.

Now that I spilled out my thoughts, let's see what others have written.
And again, it's a great movie. Worth watching.
