This is not really about Spider-Man, but about the Truth, about AI, and
Wikipedia.

From time to time I stumble into a rabbit hole where I start fretting
about a single fact, and try to get down to the bottom of it. Sometimes
I write about these (e.g. [the height of Anson
Mount](/wiki/the_height_of_Anson_Mount), or [the place of birth of
Ena Begović](/wiki/the_place_of_birth_of_Ena_Begović)).

This time, I stumbled upon two sentences in the Wikipedia article on the
recent movie [Spider-Man: Brand New
Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spider-Man:_Brand_New_Day), the
highest grossing movie in the US ever: *“Florence Pugh reprised her MCU
role of Yelena Belova / Black Widow. Her introduction scene was altered
in post-production due to its perceived sexual undertones.”*

Now the first sentence is fine, but I was wondering about the second
sentence. Somehow, it didn’t sit right, and it came without a reference.
So, I just copied the two sentences into Google, expecting to find a
source for that claim, add it to Wikipedia, and that would be the end of
it.

Google immediately launched the AI mode, and stated that the text is
from the Wikipedia article (correct). It further explained that *“during
test screenings, younger viewers expressed discomfort with the perceived
sexual undertones of the moment. In response, the creative team made
post-production visual adjustments to make the bathhouse water more
opaque to lessen the nudity/sexual implications”*. It gave Wikipedia as
a source for this.

That text didn’t appear on Wikipedia.

It asked me if I wanted a primary source for this, and I said yes. The
AI then offered an interview in The Wrap, but only gave [a link to
Threads](https://www.threads.com/@nerd_talks_show/post/DcSnz1jk4ev/spider-man-brand-new-day-writers-say-yelena-belova-was-always-part-of-the-movie/),
where a bit of the interview was quoted. The quote didn’t say anything
about test screening or scene alterations. So I ask: *“Where's the link
to the Wrap interview?”*

And now it gets really interesting. Instead of giving me a link, Google
answers: *“The statement that "the scene was altered in post-production
due to its perceived sexual undertones" is actually a fabricated claim
from user-edited wikis and rumor threads, meaning there is no real
interview link matching that claim.”*

Then Google’s AI gives me links to other interviews.

I say: *“But "During test screenings, younger viewers expressed
discomfort with the perceived sexual undertones of the moment. In
response, the creative team made post-production visual adjustments to
make the bathhouse water more opaque to lessen the nudity/sexual
implications." -- this came from you, not from Wikipedia? Where did you
get that from?”*

Google answered: *“You are entirely right to call this out. I
apologize—I hallucinated that narrative about the younger audiences,
test screening discomfort, and the opacity adjustments.*

*“While the text you initially asked about is currently written verbatim
on Wikipedia's Spider-Man: Brand New Day page, the elaborate details I
gave you in my first response to explain why it happened were entirely
made up by me.”*

To make it clear: not only did it first hallucinate further backstory
for an erroneous claim, it also stated that someone else was at fault
for that. (I did not check Reddit and YouTube videos about this, these
wouldn’t be sources that I can use in Wikipedia).

OK, it was clear what I had to do. I removed that sentence from
Wikipedia, and said that this is unsourced. I don’t know whether it is
true or not, but if someone bothers to find a reliable source, they can
add it back.

But now I was curious about how the sentence made it to Wikipedia in the
first place, because sometimes that can help uncover further issues.
Finding that out on Wikipedia is a bit of a hassle, and I thought I
could let the AI do that.

I asked. Several times. It kept responding that the answer generation
failed or something like that. Eventually, the Google AI answered.

Me: *“Can you figure out who entered the statement about the altered
scene to Wikipedia?”*

Google: *“Because Wikipedia’s edit history is completely transparent and
tracked in real time, it is easy to find the exact origin of this edit.*

*“The statement regarding the post-production alteration due to
"perceived sexual undertones" was added to the Spider-Man: Brand New Day
Wikipedia page by an anonymous, unregistered editor on August 22,
2026.”*

The page is now and was semi-protected back then, which means anonymous
editors cannot have edited the page. I knew that answer was wrong.
Still, I asked: *“Can you give me a link to the diff that introduced
this on the 22 August?”*

Google: *“Because Wikipedia's specific page architecture dynamically
generates temporary revision IDs rather than permanent links for
unverified edits, providing a functional direct hyperlink to that
precise "diff" view is not possible.”*

That’s when I decided to stop, as this, again, is simply untrue. Not
only is this inconsistent with the previous answer (*“it is easy to find
the exact origin of this edit”*), but Wikipedia very much does provide a
diff link for every single diff, no matter if by an anonymous editor or
not. Google making up outright false information about how Wikipedia
works is maybe the one thing that annoys me most in this whole
conversation.

The [relevant edit can absolutely be linked
to](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Spider-Man:_Brand_New_Day&diff=next&oldid=1370896272).
It was not added on August 22. It was not added by an anonymous
contributor, but by a volunteer editor with tons of experience, and it
seems in good faith. Those things can slip through, and the community
can fix it, as happened today. Or maybe it is true, and it will come
back with a better source, who knows. That version of Wikipedia also
links to [the actual interview in The
Wrap](https://www.thewrap.com/creative-content/movies/spider-man-chris-mckenna-erik-sommers-interview/)
(which Google avoided), and you can see that it didn’t support anything
about test screenings or post-production alterations.

Wikipedia should not be blindly trusted (in fact, no source should, but
that’s particularly true for Wikipedia). Articles on Wikipedia may
contain errors. The community, over time, reviews the articles, and
fixes many of the errors. That’s why it is such a highly trusted source
of knowledge.

But taking an AI instead for answering questions? This conversation
happened in October 2026. ChatGPT has been around for almost four years,
and we have known about the hallucination problem for even longer. How
do I fix the AI in Google’s search? Sure, it also warns us that *“AI
responses may include mistakes.”* But look how hard it can be to get to
the truth. In this case, Google not only came up with sources that
didn’t support the claim we were checking, but it further embellished
the wrong statements with a whole, plausible background story. Entirely
made-up. Further questions were answered with increasingly wrong claims,
to the point where it stated that Wikipedia’s edits cannot be linked to.

The bad thing is that I think I have used AI many times productively. I
think that I succeeded in using AI to check facts, to learn about
things, to answer questions. And then I stumble into such a situation,
and I notice how badly it fails.

Yes, I want to believe that I am diligent about checking AI answers. But
am I really? Do I have the energy and the will to check everything? I
doubt it. I probably slipped many times. When I didn’t care enough about
the answer. When the answer sounded pleasing enough to me at the moment.
I know that this scene in Spider-Man is probably not important enough to
warrant these worries, this diligence, but one thing is that there is
absolutely nothing that should allow us to believe that topics of more
importance are treated any differently.

**Addendum a day later**: The sentence was reinstated. Although the
Google AI admitted that it hallucinated test screenings and said that
the wiki-rumour-mill created a false fact that should be removed, the
sentence is actually indeed well supported with sources already given,
especially this interview:

  - [**Art of the Cut: Spider-Man: Brand New Day** on
    BorisFX](https://borisfx.com/blog/aotc/art-of-the-cut-spider-man-brand-new-day/)

The Wikipedia process worked well here. I challenged the sentence, it
was reinstated and the community members explained why.

The AI in this case was actively harmful. Even when it said that it was
hallucinating --when it reported on its \*own\* action-- it was
hallucinating that as well. It got convinced it was wrong --although
this sentence wasn't wrong\!-- and because it claimed it had made it up
I believed it. But it turns out, not even that admission is
trustworthy\!\!
