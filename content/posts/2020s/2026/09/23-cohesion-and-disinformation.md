I guess that by now there is little doubt that the Russian and other
governments, such as Iran, are having massively successful
(dis)information campaigns on social media, in the countries of Europe
and the US, and likely many others too.

Their effectiveness is due to exploiting real existing disagreements in
society, magnifying them, and blowing them up way beyond proportion.
This ensures that society becomes further split, in fact so far that,
amidst a booming economy and record low crime and unemployment rates,
the fear of actual civil war and violent unrest is as high as it has not
been for a century. The common ground is being eroded, and topics of low
impact on the actual day to day life, such as the small number of
immigrants to the EU or the tweets of the US president dominate the
daily discourse, so that high-impact topics such as climate change or
demographic change and their economic consequences are not even on our
agenda.

In fact, calling them disinformation campaigns is highly misleading,
because they are most effective if they are actually informative and
correct. They are most effective if they pick up the real fears
(unfounded or not) of real people, and magnify them. This forces the
ongoing discourse, and puts the whole society into an ongoing reactive
mode. All of us just react, breathlessly, to the latest shocking moment
instead of focusing on sustained growth and improvement.

The picture above shows how foreign agents form the cores of the
conversation. Note, this is not only on the conservative side, but also,
and actually in an even bigger amount, on the progressive side. The
researcher who created this picture points out that it felt weird to
consider these accounts as equally problematic, because on his side they
were resonating with him so much.

*That. Is. The. Effing. Point.*

Rarely do these agents have an explicit goal. I doubt they are attacking
Star Wars because they want to see the movie tank, or because of a
direct political goal. Their goal is to increase chaos, weaken
coherence, reduce the ability to act. Sometimes the goals are more
obvious — for example the operations in Macedonia and Greece, aiming at
keeping NATO from growing further — but most of them are not. They might
be just aiming at growing discord, or at training people doing this kind
of operations.

The people leading these operations are likely of varying intelligence,
but all of them will gather years and decades of experience in using
social media for manipulation. Also, the operations are cheap and often
not even illegal, most often entirely risk free.

We shouldn’t even be surprised about the existence of these operations.
The West has used similar methods for decades: emitting radio signals
into the communist countries is just the best known. The US is
complaining about interference into their elections? Given some
operations of the CIA such complaints are almost amusing. The most
surprising thing would be if the US or France would \*not\* have similar
operations ongoing. That would be almost negligent.

Why are they doing this? How would I know? My best guess is that from
the point of view of Russia or Iran or North Korea the West had
accumulated such a large advantage that their most effective bet to ever
return to a more level playing field is to drag the West down so hard,
to break us into factions, to make us betray our deepest values, with
low-cost, no-risk social media operations. What are we gonna do? Impose
sanctions because DeplorableJenny37369 made a retweet from a Russian IP
address?

Again — this doesn’t mean that they are not real people with valid and
different points of views involved. But their concerns are being
hijacked, magnified, abused. Read the case studies in the references
below to see these mechanisms at work. And this happens on all sides. I
am sure I have fallen for these campaigns, just as basically anyone
else.

How can we react?

I wish I knew. A few ideas, none of them promising.

**Can we all just become more resilient to these operations?** Given
that these operations are run by highly trained people who dedicate
their lives to social media, I guess the chances that we all get
collectively smarter and wiser about all of this are not good. You see,
I don’t assume that I can beat up an active Marine, so why should I
assume that I will constantly outsmart an active intelligence agent.

**Technological solutions that detect and shut down such operations?**
If we expect them to happen voluntarily by the tech companies — I doubt
this to happen. I doubt Twitter has the capability or incentive to spent
the resources required to counter such measures for something like the
Macedonian market. Could regulation compel them? How would that
regulation look like? Also, almost all measures will be countered by
more sophisticated operations. This is an arms race where the defense is
much more expensive than the attack, in many different ways.

**Give up some of the level of anonymity we currently enjoy on the
Web.** I find this approach both terrifying and promising. I am afraid
that this is a very high price to pay. I am afraid that this might, if
not done with extreme care and massive input from those groups, be
potentially most harmful to some of our most vulnerable members in
society, such as those who are LGBTQ\*, or who suffer from diseases or
disabilities, or who are hiding from abusers, and many others. The web’s
anonymity has, again and again, been described as the space that allowed
them to breathe, to live, to learn how to be themselves. And no matter
what the solution is — pseudonymity? asymmetric identity flows? — this
has to be preserved. But this is so dangerous, so easy to get wrong. But
at the same time there seems a space of potential solutions between the
current one and one of full transparency. (Nevermind that for certain
actors, such as the NSA, this anonymity is mostly wishful thinking on
our parts anyway)

And whereas I believe that a possible solution lives in that space, I am
also aware of the horrifying fact that one point in this space is
exactly what most of the intervening foreign powers want and are
building: a web in which the anonymity of the individual has
disappeared. By moving ourselves this way we weaken our own position in
the argument against such a controlled web.

**What about a solution like SOLID?** I am afraid that in fact such a
decentralized solution will weaken the technical possibilities to detect
and prevent such operations even more. As much as I’m hoping for SOLID
to succeed for many different reasons, I’m afraid it might also lead us
further into a world of populists and fractured societies.

**Just switch Facebook and Twitter off?** It will be back, probably
faster and more nimble than before.

**Factcheckers?** As if facts mattered too much in these discussions.
Some research shows that showing fact checking results next to false
claims increases the appeal of these claims in some cases.

**Increase the coherence of society.** In my opinion this is the most
promising path forward. And the way to do that is to improve everyone’s
life and reduce the growing gap between rich and poor. If we reduce the
economic stress many people feel, they will be more open to people who
are different. If people don’t think that they can barely make it, that
they are in desperate need of financial support by the government, they
might be more open to the idea to use government funds to help refugees.
If they realize that the world is not a zero sum game, they might be
more open to change and a fairer representation. The world is getting
better and richer. But the returns of these improvements are being
handed out very unevenly.

*The more cohesion we have, the fewer splits and cracks there already
are in society, the smaller the chances of operations succeeding that
aim to exploit these cracks and splits.*

Which basically means we’re likely screwed, because I don’t see a
realistic chance to get there. Also, because the path to this world is
being actively blocked by the disinformation campaigns that prompted
this text, which has been going on for far too long already. I am sorry
for not having a solution, for not even having a good idea of the
problem. But there is a problem that needs a solution, a problem that is
stubborn and wicked and hard.

And yet, we must. We must. We must.
