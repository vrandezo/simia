When I was a teenager I was far too much fascinated by the
[Illuminati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illuminati). Much less about
the actual historical order, and more about the memetic complex, the
[trilogy by Shea and
Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Illuminatus!_Trilogy), the
[card game by Steve
Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illuminati_\(game\)), the secret
society and esoteric knowledge, the [Templar
Story](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knights_Templar), Holy Blood of
Jesus, the rule of 5, the secret of 23, all the literature and
offsprings, etc etc...

Eventually I went to actual order meetings of the
[Rosicrucians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosicrucian_Fellowship),
and learned about some of their "secret" teachings, and also read
[Eco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umberto_Eco)'s [Foucault's
Pendulum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foucault's_Pendulum). That, and
access to the Web and eventually Wikipedia, helped to "cure" me from
this stuff: Wikipedia allowed me to put a lot of the bits and pieces
into context, and the (fascinating) stories that people like [Shea &
Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Illuminatus!_Trilogy) or [von
Däniken](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erich_von_Däniken) or [Baigent,
Leigh &
Lincoln](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holy_Blood_and_the_Holy_Grail)
tell, start falling apart. Eco's novel, by deconstructing the idea,
helps to overcome it.

He probably doesn't remember it anymore, but it was [Thomas
Römer](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Römer_\(Autor\)) who, many
years ago, told me that the trick of these authors is to tell ten
implausible, but verifiable facts, and tie them together with one highly
plausible, but made-up fact. The appeal of their stories is that all of
it seems to check out (because back then it was hard to fact check
stuff, so you would use your time to check the most implausible stuff).

I still understand the allure of these stories, and love to indulge in
them from time to time. But it was the Web, and it was learning about
knowledge representation, that clarified the view on the underlying
facts, and when I tried to apply the methods I was learning to it, it
fell apart quickly.

So it is rather fascinating to see that one of the largest and earliest
applications of [Wikibase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikibase), the
software we developed for
[Wikidata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikidata), turned out to be
actual bona fide historians (not the conspiracy theorists) using it to
[work on the Illuminati, to catalog the letters they sent to reach
other, to visualize the flow of information through the order,
etc.](https://blog.factgrid.de/archives/1695) Thanks to Olaf Simons for
heading this project, and for this write up of their current state.

It's amusing to see things go round and round and realize that, indeed,
everything is connected.
